# Remaining scientific limitations of the simplified final pilot (v0.13.x)

These follow from the specified design, not from implementation errors.

1. **Horizon and information are confounded.** H1 is always Equal [2 2] and H6 always Unequal [3 1]. The classic directed-exploration signature (horizon-dependent change in choosing the less-observed option) and the horizon change in decision noise cannot be estimated. Any H1-vs-H6 difference is a difference between two condition bundles.
2. **The primary valence hypothesis is only partly testable.** H6-PN is now fully counterbalanced (8/8/8/8), so P(choose less-observed | less-observed Positive vs Negative) is cleanly estimable. But a general approach bias toward Positive faces (observed in the earlier pilot's H1-PN games) predicts the same pattern without any change in exploration. Attributing the effect to *directed exploration* requires the valence × horizon interaction under unequal information at both horizons (main study).
2b. **H1-PN cannot simply be subtracted from H6-PN.** H1-PN (equal information) is a useful diagnostic of positive-face preference, but because information differs between the two conditions, subtracting it does not remove the approach-bias confound. The face delivered after choosing an option may itself carry utility.
2c. **Primary observation = the first free choice.** H6-PN gives **32 first choices per participant, 8 per joint cell** (less-observed valence × reward level). Later choices in H6 follow participant-dependent sampling and are not further independent observations of the 3:1 condition; use them only in explicitly modelled learning/secondary analyses. Per-person counts alone do not determine the required number of participants.
3. **Relative valence.** In PN, "less-observed Negative" is always "more-observed Positive"; PP- and NN-unequal games are the anchors for separating avoidance of uncertain negatives from preference for familiar positives.
4. **Single, exact reward gap.** The displayed forced gap is exactly 4 in every game, so the observed ranking always matches the true ranking and exploring never improves the first choice. Participants need not discover this, but check for ceiling effects and strategy learning across sections. Noise and information bonus are weakly identified with one gap level. A forced set conditioned on an exact gap is not distributed like four independent draws (the free-choice draws are unconditioned).
5. **Effective free-choice gap is 3.757, not 4.** Truncation at 1 raises the Low mean more than the High mean (expected rounded draws: Low 18.338, High 22.095; verified by exact integration and Monte Carlo in `tools/independent_reward_gap_check.py`). Free-choice outcome SD is ≈ 7.2–7.8 rather than 8.
6. **Faces are shown with the option's valence at preview and outcome.** Valence is carried by different face identities (valence-exclusive per participant), so identity-level differences are balanced only statistically (race × gender strata; covariates only if the local validation file is used).
7. **No affective state measure** (state ratings off by decision) — effects cannot be attributed to changes in mood versus stimulus-level approach/avoidance.
8. **Aborted slots.** The approved recovery policy allows fewer than 112 usable games after a crash; aborted slots are marked and must be handled by a pre-specified analysis rule.
9. **Real-display timing is not yet verified.** Timing was verified on a headless simulator that includes the real execution time of the task code but not GPU/driver costs; run `START_AVDE_TIMING_SELFTEST` (sync tests enforced) and a human keyboard rehearsal on the lab PC before data collection.
10. **Defensible aims for this pilot:** feasibility, comprehension, response quality, manipulation salience and descriptive valence-related first-choice patterns under unequal sampling. A mechanistic study needs horizon × information crossed, several reward-evidence levels, and parameter/model recovery before sample-size planning.
